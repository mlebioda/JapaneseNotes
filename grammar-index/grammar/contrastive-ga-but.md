# But -  が

> が used to join two clauses in contrast ("X, but Y"), or to softly introduce a new topic.

## 1. But (contrast)

### Structure

が simply joins two clauses in contrast. There is no requirement that either clause be negative — most examples are affirmative+affirmative (e.g. delicious *but* expensive), just semantically contrasting.

**Register:** が is neutral-to-formal (fine in writing and via ですが／ますが in polite speech). Casual spoken equivalent: けど (also だけど, or the more formal だけれども) — used with friends, replaces が directly.

- 。。。。が、。。。 - plain form + が
- 。。。ですが、。。。
- 。。。ますが、。。。

#### Verb
- Plain + が (no だ needed) → 行くが

#### い-adjective
- Plain + が (no だ needed) → 高いが

#### な-adjective
- Plain + だ + が (だ required) → 静かだが

#### Noun
- Plain + だ + が (だ required) → 学生だが

### Examples

- 漢字の勉強は難しいですが、楽しいです（かんじ、むずか、たの）- Learning kanji is difficult but fun
- フランスのお酒(さけ)は美味(おい)しいですが、高(たか)いです - French alcohol is delicious but expensive
- 私の家は安いですが狭いです（やす、せま）- my home is cheap but small
- 明日京都に行きますが、夜に東京に戻ります（もど）- I will go to Kyoto tomorrow but I will come back to Tokyo at night
- 家(いえ)から駅(えき)までは遠(とお)いですが、バスがあります - (Even though) my house is far from the station, there is a bus
- 学校に行きましたが、先生に会いませんでした(あ) - I went to school but I did not meet the teacher

## 2. Topic introduction

### Structure

- Clause + が, ... → すみませんが.... - excuse me (with bring topic)

### Examples

- すみません**が**.... - excuse me (with bring topic)
- すみません**が**。。。すずきさんはいませんか - excuse me, is Suzuki here? (bring topic)
- 昨日あげた本ですが、もう読みましたか（きのう、よ) - Have you read the book I gave you yesterday? (bring topic)
- こんにちは、ABCグローバル株式会社（かぶしきがいしゃ）の安藤（あんどう）ですが、山田（やまだ）さんはこちらにいますか。 - Hello, I am Ando from ABC Global Co., Ltd. Is Mr. Yamada here? (bring topic, introduction first then question)
- 自転車（じてんしゃ）で来（き）ましたが、自転車（じてんしゃ）でそのレストランに行（い）ってもいいですか。 - I came by bicycle, can I go to that restaurant by bicycle? (bring topic, first introduced mean of transport, then question)

## Notes

- けど is the casual equivalent of this が (see Register below) and requires だ before な-adjectives/nouns: 暇だけど, not 暇けど — い-adjectives and verbs attach directly, e.g. このケーキは美味(おい)しいけど、とても高(たか)い。 (This cake is delicious, but very expensive.)
- けど doesn't always mark a strict contrast — it can also trail off softly to hedge or hint at more without stating it directly, similar to Use Case 2's topic-introducing が above: 今(いま)から出(で)かけるけど、すぐ戻(もど)るね。 (I'll go out now, but I'll come right back.) A: 明日(あした)ひま？B: ひまだけど、どうしたの？ (A: Are you free tomorrow? B: I am, but what's up?)

## Register
Register: tiered (neutral-to-formal が, casual けど, formal けれども／だけれども)
が itself is register-flexible (works in both polite and plain sentences, especially via ですが／ますが). Casual speech typically prefers けど instead; written/formal Japanese prefers けれども／けれど. The topic-introducing use (すみませんが...) is itself a common polite cushioning device, frequent in business Japanese.

## Often confused with
- [Nは + なadj で - AND](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/naadj-de-and-connecting-adjectives) — test: Is だ dropped (→ で, meaning "and"), or kept before が (→ だが, meaning "but")?
