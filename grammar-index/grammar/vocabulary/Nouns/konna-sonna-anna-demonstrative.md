# like this, this kind of - こんな, そんな, あんな

## Structure

こんな／そんな／あんな＋N — no に before a noun (see the separate SO/degree entry for the ＋に adverbial use before a verb/adjective).

- こんな＋N – near the speaker, "this kind of ~ (close to me)"
- そんな＋N – near the listener, "that kind of ~ (close to you)" — also very commonly used anaphorically, referring back to something just said rather than a physically nearby object, e.g. そんなことないです ("that's not true") as a modest, deflecting reply to a compliment
- あんな＋N – far from both speaker and listener, "that kind of ~ (over there / like that)"

Part of the broader こそあど demonstrative series (これ／それ／あれ、この／その／あの、こう／そう／ああ、こんな／そんな／あんな) — the same near-speaker / near-listener / far-from-both distinction runs through all four rows.

## Examples

- こんな人になりたいです（ひと）- I want to become a person like this.
- そんな人はいません - There is no such kind of person.
- あんな車に乗ってみたいです（の）- I want to try riding a car like that.
