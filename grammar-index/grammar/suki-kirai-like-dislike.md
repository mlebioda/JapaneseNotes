# 好き/嫌い - like/dislike

> 好き (like) / 嫌い (dislike) — な-adjectives where the liked/disliked thing is marked with が, not を.

## Structure

好き and 嫌い are な-adjectives, but the thing being liked/disliked is marked with が, not を — even though "like" takes a direct object in English. This is one of the most common learner mistakes at this level.

- Person/thing liked/disliked (noun-modifying): すき・きらい + な + N
  - 好きな人 - person I like
  - 嫌いな果物（きら、くだもの）- fruit I dislike
- N + が + すき・きらい です - I (dis)like something
  - 食べ物が好きです - I like food
  - 魚が嫌いです（さかな、きら) - I don't like fish
- N1 は N2 + が 好き/きらい です - N1 (dis)likes N2
  - 山田さんはカラオケが好きです - Yamada likes karaoke
- Intensified: 大好き（だいすき）/ 大嫌い（だいきらい）- like/dislike very much
  - 私は横浜が大好きです（よこはま）- I like Yokohama very much
  - 彼は野菜が大嫌いです（かれ、やさい、だいきらい） - he dislikes vegetables very much
- Negation register (好き + negation): ではありません = most formal/careful; じゃありません = common polite speech; じゃない = casual/plain
  - 好きではありません／好きじゃありません／好きじゃない

## Examples

- 彼は魚が好きではありません（さかな）- He doesn't like fish
- 私は苦いものが大嫌いです（にが、だいきらい）- I hate bitter things
- 好きな人 - person I like
- 嫌(きら)いな果物(くだもの) - fruit I dislike
- 私は横浜が大好きです（よこはま）- I like Yokohama very much
- 彼(かれ)は野菜(やさい)が大嫌(だいきらい)いです - he dislikes vegetables very much

## Register
Register: tiered (formal/careful: ではありません; common polite: じゃありません; casual/plain: じゃない)
The affirmative forms (好きです／嫌いです) are neutral and usable anywhere; the three negation forms above track the same formal-to-casual scale as the copula's own negative forms.

## Often confused with
- [Be good/poor at 上手・下手](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/be-good-poor-at-jouzu-heta) — test: Is this about preference/liking something (→ 好き／嫌い), or about skill level at doing something (→ 上手／下手)?
