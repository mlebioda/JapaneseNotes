# んじゃない - when someone asks for an opinion and the speaker is sure of their idea

> A confirmation-seeking question the speaker already holds a view on — "isn't it X?" effectively means "it's X, right?"

## Structure

### Verb
- Past: V「た」+ んじゃない → A: 田中さんは今日来(こ)ないのですか。(たなか, きょう, こ) B: 田中さんは大阪(おおさか)に出張(しゅっちょう)に行ったんじゃない？(たなか, おおさか, しゅっちょう, い) - A: Won't Mr. Tanaka come today? B: Didn't he go to Osaka on business?

### い-adjective
- Present: Adj + んじゃない → 果物(くだもの)のほうがいいんじゃない？ - Don't you think the fruit is better?

んじゃない — as a question: seeks the listener's agreement with a view the speaker already holds. (いい)んじゃない — as a response: agrees/affirms, roughly equivalent to いいよね ("yeah, good, right?")

## Examples

- 果物(くだもの)のほうがいいんじゃない？  - Don't you think the fruit is better?
- A: 田中さんは今日来ないのですか。(たなか, きょう, こ) B: 田中さんは大阪に出張に行ったんじゃない？(たなか, おおさか, しゅっちょう, い) - A: Won't Mr. Tanaka come today? B: Didn't he go to Osaka on business?

## Use Cases

んじゃない is grammatically a negative (じゃない) attached after the explanatory ん, but pragmatically functions as a positive, confirmation-seeking question.

## Notes

Comparison with と思いますか (a separate, not-yet-extracted pattern):
- んじゃない — the speaker already has a view and is seeking confirmation.
  > 果物のほうがいいんじゃない？ — "Fruit is better, don't you think?" The speaker leans toward fruit. Casual, confident nudge.
- と思いますか — the speaker is genuinely asking what the listener thinks, neutral.
  > 果物のほうがいいと思いますか？ — "Do you think fruit is better?" No implied lean. Polite, open question.

んじゃない = "I think X, agree?" / と思いますか = "What do you think?"

## Register

Register: tiered (casual: んじゃない; polite: んじゃないですか)
んじゃない is the plain/casual form, used with friends and family; adding ですか gives the polite equivalent んじゃないですか, suitable for more formal conversation while keeping the same confirmation-seeking nuance.

## Often confused with

- [でしょう - seems / right](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/deshou-seems-right) — test: Is the speaker seeking the listener's agreement with a view they already hold (んじゃない), or simply stating a confident guess without necessarily seeking agreement (でしょう)?
- [かもしれません・かもしれない - may/might](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/kamoshiremasen-kamoshirenai-may-might) — test: Is the speaker confident and seeking agreement with a view they hold (んじゃない), or expressing an uncertain possibility with no confirmation-seeking nuance (かもしれません)?
