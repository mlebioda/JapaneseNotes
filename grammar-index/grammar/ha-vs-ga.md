# は vs が

> は marks the sentence topic (what we're talking about); が marks the grammatical subject, often introducing new information.

## Structure

| は | が |
| --- | --- |
| marks the topic of the sentence (what we're talking about) | marks the grammatical subject, often new/not-yet-established information |
| often used in questions | often used in answers, or to give emphasis |

## Examples

- ホテルはどこですか - Where is the hotel? (は - asking about a known topic, "the hotel")
- どこがホテルですか - Which one is the hotel? (が - asking to identify the unknown subject among options)

## Notes

- Interrogative words (だれ, なに, どこ, どれ, etc.) always take が when they are the subject, never は — e.g. だれが来ますか (not だれは来ますか).
- This subject-marking が is unrelated to the が covered under conjunctions/BUT (the "but" conjunction) — same word, two different grammatical roles.

## Register
Register: neutral
は and が are grammatical particles used at every formality tier — politeness is carried by the sentence's predicate/copula, not by the particle choice itself.
