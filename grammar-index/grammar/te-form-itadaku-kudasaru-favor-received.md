# Vて形 + いただく（謙譲語）・くださる（尊敬語） - describing a favor received from a superior

> Describing a favor done by someone of higher status, using either humble いただく (from the receiver's viewpoint) or respectful くださる (from the benefactor's viewpoint)

## 1. いただく — kenjougo, receiver's viewpoint

### Structure

### Verb
- Aは Bに Vて形 + いただきます (= もらいます; A = receiver, B = benefactor) → 社長(しゃちょう)に会議(かいぎ)に出席(しゅっせき)していただきました - The president was kind enough to attend the meeting

## 2. くださる — sonkeigo, benefactor's viewpoint

### Structure

### Verb
- Bは Aに Vて形 + くださる (= くれます; A = receiver, B = benefactor) → 先輩(せんぱい)はランチを奢(おご)ってくださいました - My senior treated me to lunch

## Examples

手紙(てがみ)をいただきました - I received a letter
社長(しゃちょう)に会議(かいぎ)に出席(しゅっせき)していただきました - The president was kind enough to attend the meeting
お客様に お名前を 書いて いただきます (きゃくさま、か) - Could you (customer) write your name, please?
ケーキをくださいました - I was given cake
先輩(せんぱい)はランチを奢(おご)ってくださいました - My senior treated me to lunch

## Notes

Easy to mix up which side is the grammatical subject: with いただく, the speaker/receiver (A) is the subject ("I received the favor of B doing V"); with くださる, the benefactor (B) is the subject ("B did V for me"). Both describe the same real-world event from opposite grammatical viewpoints.

## Register
Register: tiered (kenjougo いただく vs sonkeigo くださる)
Both are formal/polite-context constructions, but pick opposite grammatical viewpoints — いただく humbles the speaker's reception, くださる elevates the benefactor's action. Used toward or about superiors, customers, and outsiders.
