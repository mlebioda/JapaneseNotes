# ている → てる / ていた → てた (casual contraction)

> In casual spoken Japanese, ている/ていた are commonly contracted to てる/てた by dropping the い.

## Structure

| Formal | Casual | Meaning |
|--------|--------|---------|
| 知っている | 知ってる | to know |
| やっている | やってる | to be doing |
| 言っていた | 言ってた | was saying / used to say |
| やっていた | やってた | was doing / used to do |
| 勉強していた | 勉強してた | was studying |
| 遊(あそ)んでいる | 遊(あそ)んでる | to be playing |

The same "drop the い" rule applies equally to で-form verbs (nu/bu/mu group: 読んでいる→読んでる, 死んでいる→死んでる), not just って-form ones.

## Examples

- クラブ活動(かつどう)でやっていたよ。→ やってたよ。 - I had played basketball as a club activity. (よ adds an assertive/informing tone here — "you know")

## Use Cases

The **よ** particle at the end adds an assertive/informing tone — "you know" or "I'll have you know." This contraction is casual speech only — avoid in formal writing or polite situations.

## Notes

**Related contraction:** ておく→とく (e.g. 買っておく→買っとく — "buy it in advance") follows a different pattern (drops お, not い) but is commonly learned alongside this one.

## Register

Register: tiered (casual / formal)
てる／てた is casual-speech only — appropriate with friends, family, or informal writing. Formal writing and polite conversation require the uncontracted ている／ていた.

## Often confused with

- [V「て形」おります - Humble progressive / state form](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/vteorimasu-humble-progressive-state-form) — test: is this casual speech about anyone (drop the い: てる), or humble/kenjougo speech about your own actions to a superior (おります)?
- [V「て形」いらっしゃいます - Respectful (尊敬語) progressive/state form](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/vteirasshaimasu-respectful-progressive-state-form) — test: is this casual speech about anyone (drop the い: てる), or respectful/sonkeigo speech about a superior's actions (いらっしゃいます)?
