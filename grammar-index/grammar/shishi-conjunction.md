# 。。。し、。。。し - conjunction

> Lists two or more descriptions or reasons about one topic, implying the list isn't exhaustive — more reasons exist but aren't stated.

## Structure

### Verb
- V(dict/た) + し → 佐藤(さとう)くんは足(あし)をけがした**し**、あまり動(うご)けないよね。 - Mr. Sato injured his foot, so he can't move that much.

### い-adjective
- Adj + し → うちは駅(えき)から遠(とお)い**し**、部屋(へや)もせまい**し**、ひっこしをしたいと思(おも)っています - My house is far from the station and the room is small, so I think I want to move

### な-adjective
- Adj + だ + し → この靴(くつ)、色(いろ)もきれいだ**し**、サイズもちょうどいい**し**、これにします。 - These shoes' color looks nice and the size is just right, so I will buy them.

### Noun
- N + だ + し → 明日数学のテストだし、わたしは数学が苦手だし、今日は家で復習します。(あした, すうがく, すうがく, にがて, きょう, いえ, ふくしゅう) - Tomorrow is a math test, and I am not good at math, so I will review at home.

Attach し to every item in the list.

Each し-clause = one reason/trait, equal weight. し itself already signals "this list isn't exhaustive — more exist."

Decide も per item independently. も is NOT tied to a fixed position (not "always item 2"). Attach it to whichever item you want to mark as "this one too, on top of the others."
- No も on an item → stated plainly.
- も on an item → explicitly flags it as one of several similar things.
- も can go on the first item, all items, some items, or none — it's about which items you tag, not where they sit in the list.

Typical patterns (usage tendencies, not rules):
- も on every item → emphasizes a shared category of traits. アイツはお金もないし、才能もない。(That guy has no money and no talent either.)
- も on the first item only → "and this isn't even the main reason." 天気もいいし、ピクニックに行かない？(The weather's nice too — want to go on a picnic?)
- も on none → neutral, plain listing. Still fully grammatical.

## Examples

- ジャパンホテルは部屋(へや)が広(ひろ)い**し**、食事(しょくじ)も美味(おい)しい**し**、良(い)いホテルだと思(おも)います - The rooms are big and the meals are delicious so I think Japan Hotel is a good hotel
- A: 佐藤くん、風邪は大丈夫ですか。(さとう, かぜ, だいじょうぶ) B: 熱はもう下がった**し**、のども痛くない**し**、大丈夫です。(ねつ, さ, いた, だいじょうぶ) - A: Mr. Sato, how is your cold? B: My fever is gone, and I don't have a sore throat anymore, so I am fine.
- この靴(くつ)、色(いろ)もきれいだ**し**、サイズもちょうどいい**し**、これにします。 - These shoes' color looks nice and the size is just right, so I will buy them.
- うちは駅(えき)から遠(とお)い**し**、部屋(へや)もせまい**し**、ひっこしをしたいと思(おも)っています - My house is far from the station and the room is small, so I think I want to move
- 明日数学のテストだし、わたしは数学が苦手だし、今日は家で復習します。(あした, すうがく, すうがく, にがて, きょう, いえ, ふくしゅう) - Tomorrow is a math test, and I am not good at math, so I will review at home.
- 佐藤(さとう)くんは足(あし)をけがした**し**、あまり動(うご)けないよね。 - Mr. Sato injured his foot, so he can't move that much.
- ごめん、今日飲み会に行けないわ。これから、仕事もある**し**…。(きょう, の, かい, い, しごと) - Sorry, I can't go to the drinking party today. I have work to do...

## Use Cases

Used to give two or more descriptions about one topic (description-list, characterizes one subject, no conclusion required), or to list causes leading to a conclusion (reason-list, softer than から/ので, implies unstated reasons too). A single し-clause (no second item) is valid on its own — implies "and there's more I'm not saying."

## Register

Register: neutral
し attaches to the plain form of each item; the sentence's final verb carries the politeness level (plain or です／ます), independent of し itself.

## Often confused with

- [。。。ので - because](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/node-because) — test: Is this a non-exhaustive list of multiple reasons/qualities (→ し...し), or one direct reason stated plainly (→ ので)?
