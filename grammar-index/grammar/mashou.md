# Vます形＋ましょう　- Let's...

> A more direct proposal than ませんか, made when the listener is expected to accept, or used to accept someone else's offer with willingness.

## Structure

### Verb
- V ます形＋ましょう → 行く → 行きましょう (let's go)
- V ます形＋ましょう → 待つ → 待ちましょう (let's wait)

## Examples

- 日曜日の七時にしんじゅく駅で会いましょう（しちじ、えき、あ）- Let's meet at 7:00 on Sunday at Shinjuku Station
- 明日 いっしょに 学校に行きましょう（あした、がっこう) - Let's go to school together tomorrow.
- おおさかじょうに行きましょう - Let's go to Osaka Castle
- A: 明日カラオケに行きませんか　B：行きましょう！ - Would you like to go to karaoke tomorrow? B: Let's go!
- いっしょに 昼ごはんを食べませんか　B：はい、食べましょう（ひる）- A: Would you like to eat lunch with me? B: Yeah, let's eat lunch together

## Use Cases

Inviting people to do something, assuming they will accept — more direct than ませんか, which leaves the choice open; best used with people confident to accept, or a leader addressing a group. Also used to accept another person's offer with more willingness than a plain "yes."

## Register

Register: tiered (polite ます-based vs. casual plain volitional)
Polite (ます-based), but pragmatically bolder than ませんか since it assumes agreement — best with people confident to accept, or a leader addressing a group. Casual equivalent for close friends: plain volitional form (行こう, 待とう).

## Often confused with

- [Vます形＋ませんか　Shall we? / Would you like? / Why don't you...?](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/masenka) — test: does the sentence assume the listener will agree (ましょう) or leave the choice open to them (ませんか)?
- [Vます形＋ましょうか - Shall I/We](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/mashouka) — test: is the speaker proposing a shared action assuming agreement (ましょう) or deferentially offering to do something for the listener (ましょうか)?
