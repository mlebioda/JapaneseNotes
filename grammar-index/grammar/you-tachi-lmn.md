# YOU + tachi - l.mn

> Second-person pronouns ("you") and their register/formality levels, plus third-person reference.

## Structure

- あなた - you (can sound blunt/too direct toward superiors or strangers) — polite convention is to use the person's name + さん instead of あなた
- おたく, そちら - you (polite/formal, but not equivalent: おたく = direct address between equals, a bit dated; そちら = indirect "your side," typical in business/phone Japanese)
- きみ - you (casual/affectionate — used by a man toward a lover, or toward someone of equal/lower status; not used toward superiors)
- お前（おまえ）also exists as a rougher, more informal "you" than きみ — not covered in this lesson yet

Register (use / avoid):
- あなた - can sound blunt to superiors/strangers; name + さん is safer
- おたく - polite but dated; used between equals
- そちら - indirect/business register ("your side"); common on the phone
- きみ - casual; only to equals/subordinates or a partner; never to superiors

Third person: rather than a pronoun, use name + さん.
- あの人 - that person/he/she, no gender
- 彼（かれ）ら・たち - he
- 彼女（かのじょ）ら・たち - she

## Register
Register: tiered (polite/formal: おたく／そちら; neutral-but-risky: あなた; casual: きみ)
あなた can sound blunt toward superiors or strangers, so name + さん is generally safer; おたく and そちら are politer but stylistically different (おたく is a bit dated between-equals usage, そちら is the indirect business/phone register); きみ is casual, used only toward equals, subordinates, or a partner, never toward superiors.
