# まるで - just like / not at all

> まるで (from 丸で, "wholly") is an intensifying adverb — it never allows a partial or mild comparison, only total resemblance or total absence.

## 1. Simile use — total resemblance

### Structure
- Before a plain predicate: まるで + N/clause + ようだ／みたいだ → まるで夢(ゆめ)のようだ - it's just like a dream
- Before a noun: まるで + N + ような／みたいな → まるで夢(ゆめ)のような話(はなし) - a story just like a dream
- Before a verb: まるで + ように／みたいに → まるで飛(と)ぶように走(はし)った - ran as if flying
- Polite: まるで + ようです／みたいです → まるでアメリカ人(じん)のようです - it's just as if he were American

### Examples

- あの人(ひと)は英語(えいご)がとても上手(じょうず)です。まるでアメリカ人(じん)のようです。 - That person is so good at English — it's just as if he were American

## 2. Negation use — total absence

### Structure
- まるで + [verb/adj]ない → まるでわからない - I don't understand at all
- まるで + 違(ちが)う → まるで違(ちが)う - completely different

## Register

Register: neutral
まるで carries no politeness of its own — formality comes from whatever follows it (ようだ vs ようです, plain vs polite verb).

## Often confused with

- [...ようです](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/you-desu) — test: Are you emphasizing total, complete resemblance, or just stating a general impression of similarity without added emphasis?
