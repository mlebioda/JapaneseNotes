# OR - か、か

> Presents two alternatives — "A or B" / "whether A or B" — using か after each option.

## Structure

...か...か

- Can attach to verbs, adverbs, or nouns
- Often used with 知らない／わかりません ("don't know")

## Examples

- 今日(きょう)は九月(くがつ)八日(ようか)ですか、九日(ここのか)ですか - Is today September 8th or 9th?
- あそこは映画館(えいがかん)ですか、美術館(びじゅつかん)ですか - Is that a movie theater or an art museum?
- これはアンドさんのざっしですか、山田さんのざっしですか - Is this Mr. Ando's magazine or Mr. Yamada's magazine?
- これは日本語（にほんご）か英語（えいご）か分（わ）かりません - I don't know whether this is in Japanese or in English.
- この料理（りょうり）はおいしいかまずいか分（わ）かりません。- I don't know whether this dish is tasty or not.
- 行（い）くか行（い）かないか、まだ決（き）めていません - I still haven't decided whether I'll go or not.
- 雨（あめ）が降（ふ）るか降（ふ）らないか心配（しんぱい）です。- I'm worried about whether it will rain or not.

## Use Cases

Plain-form か…か (as in わかりません／決めていません) is register-neutral, used in both speech and writing. ですか versions are the polite question form of the same pattern.

Structurally, plain か…か is one embedded clause inside a larger sentence (行くか行かないか、まだ決めていません), while 〜ですか、〜ですか is two separate, adjacent yes/no questions offering a choice — not a single embedded clause. Both express "A or B," but only the plain form nests inside another predicate.

## Notes

か…か ("A or B" / "whether A or B") is easy to confuse with a single sentence-final question か, and with や…や (a non-exhaustive "A and B and others" listing) — all three use か／や particles but serve different functions.

## Register
Register: tiered (plain, polite)
Plain か…か (used before わかりません／決めていません) is register-neutral, appropriate in both casual speech and writing. The 〜ですか、〜ですか adjacent-question form is the polite variant, appropriate with teachers, strangers, or customers.

## Often confused with

- [かどうか - whether or not](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/kadouka-whether-or-not) — test: Is this presenting two specific concrete alternatives to choose between (か…か), or asking about the truth of a single yes/no proposition (かどうか)?
