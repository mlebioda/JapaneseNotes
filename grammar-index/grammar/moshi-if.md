# もし - if

> Adds a hypothetical, uncertain nuance to a conditional clause — signals that a conditional (ば/たら/なら) is coming and that the condition is less certain.

## Structure

- もし + [conditional clause: ば／たら／なら] → もし晴(は)れたら、出(で)かけよう。(If it clears up, let's go out.)

**With vs. without もし:**
- 晴れたら、出かけよう。— neutral condition, speaker expects it might happen
- もし晴れたら、出かけよう — more hypothetical, "if by chance it clears up..." — speaker is less certain it will happen, treating it more as a "what if"

## Examples

- もし晴(は)れたら、出(で)かけよう。 - If it clears up, let's go out.
- もしあなたが行(い)くなら、私(わたし)も行(い)く。 - If you are going, I will go too.

## Use Cases

もし marks a hypothetical, uncertain framing ("what if..."), not a neutral/likely condition — distinct from もしかしたら／もしかして ("perhaps"/"by any chance"), which express the speaker's own uncertainty about a statement rather than framing a conditional.

## Register

Register: neutral
- Used identically in casual and polite speech; no formality restriction (unlike なさい or the plain imperative, which are tied to hierarchy).

## Often confused with

- [。。。場合は, 。。。 - in case of, if](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/baai-in-case-of-if) — test: Are you flagging general hypothetical uncertainty before a ば/たら/なら clause (もし), or describing a specific circumstantial case, often in formal/procedural contexts (場合は)?
