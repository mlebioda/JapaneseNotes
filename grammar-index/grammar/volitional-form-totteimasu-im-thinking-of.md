# Volitional form + と思っています - I'm thinking of

> Used to state an intention you're currently thinking about / considering.

## Structure

### Verb
- Present: V-volitional + と思っています → 将来(しょうらい)日本(にほん)で働(はたら)こうと思(おも)っています。- I am thinking of working in Japan in the future.

## Examples

- 将来(しょうらい)日本(にほん)で働(はたら)こうと思(おも)っています。- I am thinking of working in Japan in the future.
- 来年(らいねん)結婚(けっこん)しようと思(おも)っています。- I am thinking of getting married next year.
- パーティーに鈴木(すずき)さんを誘(さそ)おうと思(おも)っています。- I am thinking of inviting Mr. Suzuki to the party.

## Use Cases

The progressive ています (rather than plain と思います) signals an intention the speaker is currently holding or has been considering for a while, not a thought occurring right at the moment of speaking.

## Register
Register: tiered (polite / casual)
と思っています (polite ます ending) is the neutral-polite form used in most conversation and writing. Dropping the copula to と思っている is the casual equivalent used with friends and family.

## Often confused with

- [V「plain」つもりです - conviction of doing something planned by speaker](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/vplaintsumoridesu-conviction-of-doing-something-planned-by-speaker) — test: Is the speaker still weighing the idea, or have they already resolved to do it? (と思っています = still considering; つもりです = resolved intention)
- [V「dict」 こと ＋ に　＋ する - I decided (on my own will)](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/v-dict-koto-ni-suru-i-decided-on-my-own-will) — test: Is this an ongoing consideration, or a firm one-time decision the speaker has just made? (と思っています = considering; ことにする = firm decision)
