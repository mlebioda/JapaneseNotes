# Particle omission — が、は、を、(に)

> が, は, を (and often に with motion verbs) are frequently dropped in casual speech.

## Structure

- When?
  - often omitted on casual occasions - more natural
  - in particular, if the speaker and listener are close
  - mainly が, は, を — and often に with motion verbs in casual speech; not every particle drops this freely (で, と, から, まで are not casually omitted the same way)

Register: casual/colloquial speech only — never in formal writing, business Japanese, or polite/careful speech, even if the sentence still ends in です/ます.

## Examples

- この映画　は　とても面白いですね（えいが、おもしろ) → この映画　とても面白いですね（えいが、おもしろ) - This movie is interesting, isn't it? (abbreviation)
- いっしょに ご飯　を　食べませんか（はん、たべ）→ いっしょに ご飯　食べませんか（はん、たべ) - would you like to eat a meal together? (abbreviation)

## Register
Register: tiered (casual: が／は／を（に）dropped; formal/careful: particles retained)
Particle omission is exclusively a casual, colloquial-speech phenomenon between people who are close — never appropriate in formal writing, business Japanese, or careful/polite speech, even if the sentence still ends in です／ます.
