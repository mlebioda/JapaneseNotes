# Would you please let me do...

> A humble, polite way to ask a superior for permission to do something.

## Structure

### Verb
- V「causative + て形」+ ください (polite but least) → 相談(そうだん)させてください。(Please let me ask for your advice.)
- V「causative + て形」+ もらえませんか (usually polite enough) → 見(み)させてもらえませんか。(Could you let me have a look?)
- V「causative + て形」+ いただけませんか (very polite) → 行(い)かせていただけませんか。(Would you please let me go?)

## Examples

- 部長(ぶちょう)、頭(あたま)が痛(いた)いので、病院(びょういん)に行(い)かせていただけませんか。 - Manager, I have a headache, would you please let me go to the hospital? (very polite)
- 新(あたら)しい絵(え)ができたと聞(き)いたんですが、見(み)させてもらえませんか。 - I heard that you have finished a new drawing, could you please let me have a look? (polite enough)
- 相談（そうだん）させてください - please let me ask for your advice (least polite)

## Use Cases

The standard humble way to ask permission *from* a superior — the exception to the plain causative form's top-down-only rule. Politeness scales from てください (least polite) through てもらえませんか to ていただけませんか (most polite).

## Register

Register: tiered
- てください — polite, but the least polite of the three causative-request forms.
- てもらえませんか — usually polite enough for asking a superior.
- ていただけませんか — very polite; the safest choice with someone significantly senior.

## Often confused with

- [Vて形＋くださいませんか politer form of くれませんか - could you please (do smth for me)](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/kudasaimasenka-polite-request) — test: Are you asking permission to do something yourself (causative + て形 + ください/もらえませんか/いただけませんか, "let me do X"), or asking the other person to do something for you (plain て形 + くださいませんか, "please do X for me")?
- [V「て」もらえませんか, V「て」くれませんか](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/v-te-moraemasenka-v-te-kuremasenka) — test: Are you asking permission to do something yourself (causative-based), or asking someone else to act on your behalf?
