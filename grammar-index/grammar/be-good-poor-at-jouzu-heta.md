# Be good/poor at 上手・下手

> 上手 (good at) / 下手 (poor at) — な-adjectives where the skill is marked with が, not を.

## Structure

上手 and 下手 are な-adjectives; the skill itself takes が, not を (same pattern as 好き/嫌い).

Register: saying you yourself are 上手 can sound boastful/arrogant in Japanese — natives mostly use 上手 to praise someone else or ask about their skill, and prefer 得意 or a humble phrasing when talking about their own ability.

- N が 上手・下手です
  - For verbs: V-る + こと + が + 上手・下手です
    - 料理を作ることが上手です（りょうり、つく）- (he/she) is good at cooking
- Negative / Past: 上手じゃないです - not good at it / 上手でした - was good at it

## Examples

- 日本語が上手ですね - good at Japanese
- 歌が下手ですね (うた) - bad at singing
- 彼女は絵が下手です（え、へた）- She is not good at drawing
- 料理を作ることが上手です（りょうり、つく）- (he/she) is good at cooking

## Register
Register: neutral
上手です／下手です carry no formality tier of their own — plain/polite is set by the copula, same as any な-adjective. The one usage caution is pragmatic rather than register-based: calling yourself 上手 can sound boastful, so natives typically reserve it for praising others and prefer 得意 or humble phrasing when describing their own skill.

## Often confused with
- [好き/嫌い - like/dislike](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/suki-kirai-like-dislike) — test: Is this about skill level at doing something (→ 上手／下手), or about preference/liking something (→ 好き／嫌い)?
