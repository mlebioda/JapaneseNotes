# ウチ・ソト (Uchi-Soto) - In-group / Out-group

> Key concept for choosing between honorific (尊敬語) and humble (謙譲語) language — the boundary is relative and shifts depending on who you're comparing yourself to (e.g., your department vs. another department in the same company, or your whole company vs. an outside client)

## Structure

ウチ (in-group, relative to current context):
- 家族（かぞく）- family
- 会社（かいしゃ）- your own company
- 友人（ゆうじん）- friends
- ... anyone else in the same group as you

ソト (out-group, relative to current context):
- 他の会社（ほかのかいしゃ）- other companies
- 家族以外（かぞくいがい）- non-family
- 知らない人（しらないひと）- strangers

## Examples

山本(やまもと)社長(しゃちょう)はいらっしゃいますか。 - Is Mr. Yamamoto (the president) in?
ただいま山本(やまもと)は外出(がいしゅつ)しております。(no honorific like さん)  - Yamamoto is out at the moment.

## Use Cases

When talking **to** or **about** ソト people, honor them (尊敬語) and speak humbly about yourself and your own ウチ group (謙譲語) — including ウチ members who outrank you within your own group.

This means: even someone senior to you inside your ウチ (your boss, your father) gets humble language, not honorific language, once the conversation involves ソト people — because relative to the ソト listener, that person is part of "your side."

**Example:** Talking to a client (ソト) about your own CEO (ウチ, but senior to you) → use kenjougo for the CEO's actions, not sonkeigo. (e.g., 社長の田中は今、外出しております, not 外出されています)

Within ウチ only (e.g., talking to a fellow employee about the same CEO), normal sonkeigo for the CEO still applies, since there's no ソト listener to consider.

## Register
Register: tiered (尊敬語 for ソト-facing referents; 謙譲語 for ウチ referents)
Not a pattern itself but the selection rule for keigo direction: honor ソト people, speak humbly about your own ウチ side — including in-group superiors — whenever a ソト listener is involved.
