# V「dict」＋な - Don't do...

> A strong prohibition — "Don't do ~" — formed from the dictionary form + な.

## Structure

### Verb
- Dictionary form + な → 行く → 行くな (Don't go!)
    -  心配する → 心配するな (Don't worry!)

## Examples

- このチームにあなたが必要(ひつよう)だ。やめるな。 - The team needs you (you are needed on the team). Don't quit.
- あそこは危険(きけん)です。行(い)くな！ - It is dangerous there. Don't go.
- 明日(あした)大事(だいじ)な試験(しけん)がある。勉強(べんきょう)を邪魔(じゃま)するなよ - Tomorrow I have an important exam. Don't interrupt me (softening)

## Use Cases

A stronger restriction than ではいけない. Impolite when used toward superiors or friends, unless in extreme emergencies. The manner of speaking can be softened by adding よ (するなよ).

## Notes

Stronger/more direct restriction than ではいけない.

## Register

Register: tiered
- 禁止形 (dictionary form + な) sits at the blunt end of the command register spectrum — the same tier as the plain imperative (書け、しろ, etc.); rude toward superiors, but usable in warnings/emergencies or casually among close (often male) friends, especially when softened with よ (するなよ).

## Often confused with

- [Vないでください - please don't do it](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/vnaidekudasai-please-dont-do-it) — test: Is the request polite/asking (ください) or blunt/commanding (plain form + な)?
- [V「て形」はいけません - is forbidden to do something](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/v-tewa-ikemasen-forbidden) — test: Is this the bluntest direct command form (辞書形＋な), or a moderate, still-somewhat-negotiable prohibition (てはいけません)?
