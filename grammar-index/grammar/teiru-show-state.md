# ている - show state

> Expresses either a resulting state after a change, or an ongoing action/habit, depending on the verb's nature.

## 1. Resulting State

### Structure

### Verb
- て形＋いる → 結婚(けっこん)しています (is married)

For punctual/instantaneous verbs (結婚する "to marry," 空く "to become free/vacant"): 結婚しています doesn't mean "is in the process of marrying," it means "is (now, as a result) married."

### Examples

- 山田さんはけっこん　しています - Mr. Yamada is married
- 来週の日曜日は、空いています（らいしゅう、あ）- I'm free next Sunday

## 2. Ongoing Action / Habit

### Structure

### Verb
- て形＋いる → 待(ま)っています (is waiting)

For durative verbs (勉強する, 待つ, 飲む): a genuinely ongoing action or a repeated habit.

### Examples

- 私は外で待っています(そと) - I'm waiting outside
- 彼女は大学で日本語を勉強しています（かのじょ、にほんご、べんきょう）- She is studying Japanese at university
- 毎日(まいにち)コーヒーを飲(の)んでいます - I drink coffee every day
- 彼女は商社で働いています（しょうしゃ、はたら）- She works for a trading company

### Past tense: ました vs ていました

Same durative verbs, but in the past — the two past forms carry a different nuance, not just a different tense:
- ました → a single completed occurrence, a plain statement of fact (regardless of how much or how long)
- ていました → was doing / was in an ongoing state or habit for a while — but implies it has since stopped or changed, and isn't the case anymore

### Examples

- 勉強(べんきょう)しました - I studied (once — a simple statement of fact, regardless of how much/how long)
- 勉強していました - I was studying (for a while, regularly) — but not anymore now
- 彼氏(かれし)に会(あ)いました - I met my boyfriend (once, a single occasion)
- 彼氏に会っていました - I was dating my boyfriend (an ongoing relationship in the past) — implies it's over now

## Register

Register: tiered (polite ています vs. plain ている vs. casual contraction てる)
Not politeness-restricted itself, but the conjugation varies by register: polite ています (as in the examples above), plain ている, casual/colloquial contraction てる (e.g. 勉強してる instead of 勉強している).

## Often confused with

- [2. V「て形」いるところです - action in progress right now](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/tokoro-desu-teiru-form) — test: is this simply an ongoing/habitual action or state (ている) or specifically emphasizing that the action is happening at this very moment (ているところです)?
