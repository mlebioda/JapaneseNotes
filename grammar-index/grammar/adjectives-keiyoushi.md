# Adjectives 形容詞（けいようし）

> The two adjective types in Japanese — い-adjectives and な-adjectives — and how each attaches to a noun.

## Structure

- い-adjective — already ends in い; used as-is directly before a noun (nothing added).
- な-adjective — add な when the adjective modifies a noun.

## Examples

- あついコーヒー - hot coffee
- 親切(しんせつ)な人(ひと) - kind person

## Notes

- A few common words end in い but are actually な-adjectives, not い-adjectives: きれい(な) - pretty/clean, きらい(な) - dislike, ゆうめい(な) - famous. They take な before a noun (きれいな部屋, not きれい部屋) and conjugate like な-adjectives, not い-adjectives.

## Register
Register: neutral
Adjective type (い vs な) is a morphological classification, not a politeness distinction — both types appear at every formality tier; only the final copula (です／だ) carries the register.
