# General counting method (1-10)

> The general native-Japanese counting series (ひとつ〜とお), used for many kinds of things when they're small, few (under 10), non-specific in shape, or when you don't remember the correct counter.

## Structure

Register: neutral — used in both casual and polite speech.

- counter + の + product
	- 今日は母は二つのリンゴを買いました
- product + を + counter
	- 母はリンゴを二つ買いました

Order: Place + に + Thing (が) + number + あります

## Examples

- 今日は母は(二つ**の**リンゴを/リンゴを２つ）買いました - Today, my mother bought 2 apples (illustrating both word orders: counter+の+product, and product+counter)
- つくえの上にりんごがいくつありますか（うえ) - How many apples are on the desk?
- 机(つくえ)の上(うえ)に りんごが 五(いつつ)つあります - There are 5 apples on the desk

## Use Cases

Used for many kinds of things, especially when they are small, few (counting under 10), non-specific in shape, or when the correct specific counter isn't known. いくつ ("how many") is the question word used with this series.

## Register
Register: neutral
Used identically in casual and polite speech — this native counting series carries no formality association of its own.

## Often confused with

- [個 (こ) - small things counter](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/ko-small-things-counter) — test: Is the count 10 or below and the item small/general in shape (→ either works, native series preferred), or above 10 (→ 個 required, native series stops at とお)?
- [Numbers](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/numbers-0-100000000) — test: Are you counting objects generically without a specific counter word (→ native series ひとつ〜とお), or just stating/reading a number itself, like a phone number or math answer (→ いち, に, さん...)?
