# Vて いただけませんか - polite requests. Politer than てください

> A kenjougo (humble) request pattern, politer than てください, used toward people superior to you.

## Structure

- Vて いただけませんか - polite requests

## Examples

- 社長(しゃちょう)、サインをしていただけませんか。 - President, could you please sign?
- 高橋先生、この漢字の読み方を教えていただけませんか。(たかはし・せんせい・かんじ・よ・かた・おし) - Mr. Takahashi, could you teach me how to read this kanji?
- 机の上を片付けていただけませんか。(つくえ/うえ/かたづ) - Would you clean your desk?

## Use Cases

いただく is the kenjougo (humble) form of もらう, humbly framing the action as a favor the speaker receives; いただける is its potential form. Although the affirmative ていただけますか is already polite, the negative-question form ていただけませんか is considered even more polite/indirect — phrasing it as "could I not receive the favor of..." leaves the listener a graceful way to decline. Not used casually among friends/family — there, てくれない？ or ～してもらえる？ is natural instead.

## Register
Register: polite-context typical
Kenjougo (humble) — appropriate toward superiors, teachers, customers, and in service/business contexts. Not used casually among friends or family.

## Often confused with
- [Vて形＋くださいませんか politer form of くれませんか - could you please (do smth for me)](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/kudasaimasenka-polite-request) — test: Is this a humble request lowering the speaker (→ ていただけませんか), or a respectful request elevating the listener (→ てくださいませんか)?
- [V「て」もらえませんか, V「て」くれませんか](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/v-te-moraemasenka-v-te-kuremasenka) — test: Is this the extra-polite kenjougo escalation (→ ていただけませんか), or the neutral-polite everyday request (→ てもらえませんか／てくれませんか)?
- [Vて形 + いただく（謙譲語）・くださる（尊敬語） - describing a favor received from a superior](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/te-form-itadaku-kudasaru-favor-received) — test: Is this asking someone to do something for you (→ ていただけませんか request), or describing a favor already received/given (→ いただく／くださる statement)?
