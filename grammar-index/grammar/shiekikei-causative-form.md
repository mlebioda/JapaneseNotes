# 使役形（しえきけい) - causative form

> Making or letting someone do something — "make [someone] do" or "let [someone] do," depending on context.

## Structure

### Verb
- Group 1 (う-verb): う→あ row + せる → 書く → 書かせる (make/let write)
- Group 2 (る-verb): drop る, add させる → 食べる → 食べさせる (make/let eat)
- Group 3 (irregular): する → させる, くる → こさせる

**Full conjugation tables:**

**Group 1 (う-verbs):** change the final う-sound to あ-sound + せる
- 書く → 書かせる (kaku → kakaseru)
- 飲む → 飲ませる (nomu → nomaseru)
- 話す → 話させる (hanasu → hanasaseru)

**Group 2 (る-verbs):** drop る, add させる
- 食べる → 食べさせる (taberu → tabesaseru)
- 見る → 見させる (miru → misaseru)

**Group 3 (irregular):**
- する → させる
- くる → こさせる

## Examples

- 買える ->  帰(かえ)らせました - to let  go back home

## Use Cases

Expresses making or letting someone do something. Plain causative (書かせる、帰らせました) is top-down only — it can't be used toward a higher-ranking person. The causative-request forms are the exception: they're the standard humble way to ask permission *from* a superior.

## Register

Register: tiered
- Plain causative (書かせる、帰らせました) — used top-down only (authority causing/letting a subordinate act: parent, teacher, boss); not used toward superiors.
- Causative-request for "let me do X" — usable toward superiors, tiered: てください (polite but least) < てもらえませんか (usually polite enough) < ていただけませんか (very polite).

## Often confused with

- [1. **動詞(どうし)の尊敬形(そんけいけい) - respectful forms of verbs**](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/sonkeikei-passive-form) — test: Are you making/letting someone perform an action (causative せる/させる), or respectfully describing what a superior does using the passive-shaped honorific (られる)?
