# N が します

> Expresses perceiving a sensory quality — a smell, sound, taste, or feeling — arising on its own.

## Structure

### Noun
- Sensory N + が + する → あの店(みせ)の近(ちか)くを通(とお)ると、いつもいいにおいがしますね。(There is always a good smell when I go by that restaurant.)

**Common collocations:**
- においがする — to have a smell / to smell of (something)
- 味がする — to taste like (something)
- 音がする — a sound occurs / can be heard
- 気分(きぶん)がする — to have a feeling / mood (physical or emotional state)
- 気(き)がする — to have a feeling/hunch that ~ (idiom, follows a plain-form clause)
	- 何(なに)か悪(わる)いことが起(お)こる気(き)がする。 - I have a feeling something bad is going to happen.

## Examples

- あの店(みせ)の近(ちか)くを通(とお)ると、いつもいいにおいがしますね。 - There is always a good smell when I go by that restaurant.
- このパンはカレーの味(あじ)がします。 - This bread tastes like curry.
- この車(くるま)、何(なに)か変(へん)な音(おと)がしませんか。 - Isn't this car making a strange noise? (less direct and certain)
- 今日(きょう)はいい気分(きぶん)がします。 - I feel good today.

## Use Cases

Often used when the speaker senses a smell, sound, voice, taste, or symptom. がする marks a sensory quality arising/being perceived (passive) — contrasts with active verbs for the same senses, e.g. 匂(にお)いを嗅(か)ぐ (to sniff/smell something on purpose) or 音(おと)を聞(き)く (to listen/hear deliberately).

## Register

Register: neutral
- Plain する / polite します is standard verb conjugation here — no hierarchy or gender coding; used identically across casual and polite speech.

## Often confused with

- [...ようです](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/you-desu) — test: Are you directly perceiving something through your senses right now (がする), or inferring/guessing based on outward appearance or indirect evidence (ようです)?
