# V「plain」そうです / そうだ - It is said that / I heard

> Reports something you heard from another source, used when the information is treated as fairly certain.

## Structure

### Verb
- Plain: V「plain」+ そうです → 天気予報(てんきよほう)を聞(き)いたんですが、明日(あした)は雪(ゆき)が降(ふ)らない**そうです**。 - I heard from the weather forecast that it won't snow tomorrow.
- Past: V「た」+ そうです → (no dedicated example in source)

### い-adjective
- い-adj + そうです → 佐藤(さとう)くんの話(はなし)では、来週(らいしゅう)の試験(しけん)は難(むずか)し**いそうです**。 - I heard from Mr. Sato that the exam next week will be difficult.

### な-adjective
- な-adj + だ + そうです → (no dedicated example in source)

### Noun
- Noun + だ + そうです → ラジさんのお父(とう)さんは大学(だいがく)の先生(せんせい)**だそうです**。 - I heard that Raj's father is a university teacher.

## Examples

- 天気予報(てんきよほう)を聞(き)いたんですが、明日(あした)は雪(ゆき)が降(ふ)らない**そうです**。 - I heard from the weather forecast that it won't snow tomorrow.
- ラジさんのお父(とう)さんは大学(だいがく)の先生(せんせい)**だそうです**。 - I heard that Raj's father is a university teacher.
- 佐藤(さとう)くんの話(はなし)では、来週(らいしゅう)の試験(しけん)は難(むずか)し**いそうです**。 - I heard from Mr. Sato that the exam next week will be difficult.
- 明日(あした)は雪(ゆき)が降(ふ)らない**そうだ**。 - I heard it won't snow tomorrow.

## Use Cases

そうです is the polite form (attached to です); dropping です gives the plain/casual equivalent そうだ.

## Register

Register: tiered (polite: そうです; plain/casual: そうだ)
そうです is the standard polite form for everyday conversation; そうだ is the casual/plain equivalent used with friends, family, or in casual writing.

## Often confused with

- [。。。らしいです - it seems](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/rashii-desu) — test: Is the speaker fairly confident in this reported information (そうです), or treating it as less certain (らしいです)?
- [...ようです](/JapaneseNotes/grammar-index/grammar/you-desu) — test: Is this something the speaker heard from someone else (そうです), or an inference the speaker is making themself from indirect evidence (ようです)?
