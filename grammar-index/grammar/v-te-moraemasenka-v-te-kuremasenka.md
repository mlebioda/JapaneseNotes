# V「て」もらえませんか, V「て」くれませんか

> Using the negative interrogative form turns a question into a polite request — politer than 「て」もらえますか and 「て」くれますか.

## Structure

| 「て」もらう | 「て」もらえる | 「て」もらえますか |
| ------ | ------- | --------- |
| 「て」くれる |         | 「て」くれますか  |

## Examples

- 来（き）てもらえませんか - could you please come (for me)?
- 本（ほん）を貸（か）して くれませんか - Could you please lend me the book?
- 明日(あした)の引(ひ)っ越(こ)し、手伝(てつだ)ってもらえませんか。 - Could you help me with moving tomorrow?
- お兄(にい)さん、明日(あした)京都(きょうと)に行(い)くんだけど、案内(あんない)してくれる？ - Brother, I will go to Kyoto tomorrow, could you guide me there?
- この漢字(かんじ)の発音(はつおん)を教(おし)えてもらえませんか(or てくれませんか)。 - Could you please teach me how to pronounce this kanji?

## Use Cases

- V「て」くれませんか sounds too formal between intimate people.
- When speaking to a superior, use 「て」いただけませんか or 「て」くださいませんか instead.
- The table's empty cell is not a gap to fill: くれる doesn't get the same potential-form step もらう does. もらえる ("can receive") is a natural, commonly used potential form that softens もらう into a request. くれられる is grammatically constructible but isn't actually used this way — requests with くれる jump straight from plain くれる to くれませんか.

## Notes

もらう and くれる belong to the giving-receiving (授受表現) verb system, which is why the two request forms differ in perspective: もらう frames it as "I receive the favor" (the requester is the subject), while くれる frames it as "they give me the favor" (the helper is the subject).

## Register
Register: tiered (casual → neutral-polite → formal)
くれる／くれない？ is casual, used between close equals; てもらえませんか／てくれませんか is the neutral-polite default for most everyday requests; ていただけませんか／てくださいませんか is the formal tier, used toward superiors, customers, and in business contexts.
