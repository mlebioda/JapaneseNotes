### STEM forms

#### while - Verb (ます-form stem) + ながら + Main action (plain form)

**ながら** describes the **background action**, while the main verb describes the **primary action**.

- **Verb (ます-form stem) + ながら + Main action (plain form)**
- The verb before **ながら**
- 音楽を聞きながら勉強します。_(Ongaku o kikinagara benkyou shimasu._) - "I study **while listening** to music.
- **Both actions must be done by the same person.**
- ❌ **先生が話しながら、私はノートを取る。** (Incorrect)
- ✅ **先生の話を聞きながら、ノートを取る。** (Correct)
- **The main action is the more important one.**
- **運転しながら電話しないでください。** (_Unten shinagara denwa shinaide kudasai._) → "Please don't talk on the phone **while driving**." (Driving is the main action, talking on the phone is secondary.)
- **ながら is only used with verbs.**
- For adjectives or nouns, use **間 (あいだ) に** instead.

### て form

#### AはＢに〜てあげる– "to do something for someone"

**Used when the speaker or someone in their group does a favor for someone else.**

> The giver is the subject; the receiver benefits from the action. 🔹 Structure: ✅ Person A は Person B に Verb (て-form) + あげる 🔹 Example Sentences:

- **私は友達に日本語を教えてあげた。** (_Watashi wa tomodachi ni Nihongo o oshiete ageta._) → "I taught my friend Japanese (as a favor)."
- **母は弟にご飯を作ってあげた。** (_Haha wa otouto ni gohan o tsukutte ageta._) → "Mom made food for my younger brother."

⚠️ **Note:** Be careful when using あげる with superiors (boss, teacher, elders). It may sound rude or arrogant.

#### Aは/が(Bに)〜てくれる (te kureru) – "someone A does something for me (or my group) B"

**Used when someone does a favor for the speaker or their in-group.**

> The receiver is the speaker; the giver does the action. 🔹 Structure: ✅ Person A が (or は) Person B (me/my group) に Verb (て-form) + くれる

🔹 **Example Sentences:**

- **友達が宿題を手伝ってくれた。** (_Tomodachi ga shukudai o tetsudatte kureta._) → **〜てくれる (te kureru) – "someone does something for me (or my group)"Used when someone does a favor for the speaker or their in-group.**
    
    > The receiver is the speaker; the giver does the action.
    
- **先生が日本語の発音を直してくれた。** (_Sensei ga Nihongo no hatsuon o naoshite kureta._) → "The teacher corrected my Japanese pronunciation (for me)." 👍 **くれる focuses on the kindness of the giver.**
    

#### AはBに〜てもらう (te morau) – "to receive a favor from someone

**Used when the speaker (or their in-group) receives a favor from someone.**

> The receiver (subject) benefits from the giver's action. 🔹 Structure: ✅ Person A は Person B に Verb (て-form) + もらう 🔹 Example Sentences:

- **私は先生に作文をチェックしてもらった。** (_Watashi wa sensei ni sakubun o chekku shite moratta._) → "I had my teacher check my essay."
- **弟は友達にゲームを貸してもらった。** (_Otouto wa tomodachi ni geemu o kashite moratta._) → "My younger brother borrowed a game from his friend."

💡 **もらう focuses on the benefit the receiver gets.**

#### XにYを〜てやる (te yaru) – "to do something for someone (lower than you)"**

**Used when the speaker does something for someone of lower status (kids, pets, plants, etc.).**

> Similar to あげる but with a sense of authority or superiority. 🔹 Structure: ✅ Person A は Person B に Verb (て-form) + やる 🔹 Example Sentences:

- **父は犬にえさをやった。** (_Chichi wa inu ni esa o yatta._) → "My dad fed the dog."
- **私は弟にゲームを買ってやった。** (_Watashi wa otouto ni geemu o katte yatta._) → "I bought a game for my younger brother." ⚠️ **やる can sound rude when used toward adults or equals. Use あげる instead.**

#### V +なら -  if 1 part not really certain is real, 2nd good "If it’s about A..." / "If A is the case..."
-  if 1 part not really certain is real, 2nd good
- Use cases:
	- if 1 part not really certain is real, 2nd good
	- Refers to a **known or supposed situation**
	- Often used when **reacting** to information or context
	- Flexible; used for suggestions, assumptions, or contrast
- Structure
	- V + なら 
		- 買い物に行くなら、コーラを買ってきてください - if you go to the shoping, buy me a coffe
	- い、な、Noun + なら
		- 高いなら、いらない
		- 
##### 。。。**なら**、。。。**がいいです**（か）- if A b is good
	- パソコンなら、秋葉原がいいですよ (あきはばら) - If it's a computer you're looking for, Akihabara is the best place.

#### short  + と  - Whenever / When [you do A], [B] always happens or happens immediately.
- Use cases
	- **Used for natural, automatic results** that follow one after another.
	- Like saying: _As soon as I looked → I saw mold._
	- The subject has no control over what happens next
	- "Whenever / When [you do A], [B] always happens or happens immediately."
- Structure:
	- V/い (ない) + と
		- 見ると、カビが生えていました (は) - When I looked, mold had grown.
	- な+ N　+ だ/ない + と

####  Vた+ ら - If A happens, then B will follow.
- Use cases
	- if a happens, b hapens - possibility of A not 100%
	- Used for **specific situations or results**.
	- Often means **"if" or "when"**, and the result depends on the condition.
	- The second action/result happens **after** the first condition is met.
	- 
- Structure
	- V stem+ たら
		- 食べたら、おなかをこわします - If I eat it, I get a stomachache.
	- い+ たら
	- な+ たら
	- N+ たら
