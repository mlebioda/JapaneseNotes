# 文法

## と - "and" / "with" (noun connector)

### N1 と N2（と N3...）- exhaustive listing, "A and B"
- Structure
	- N1 + と + N2（+ と + N3...）
- Examples
	- りんごとみかんを買いました（か）- I bought apples and oranges (a complete list — just those two, nothing else)
	- 犬と猫がいます（いぬ、ねこ）- there are a dog and a cat (and that's all there is)

### N（person）＋ と - "with" (companion/accompaniment)
- Structure
	- N（person）+ と + V
- Examples
	- 友達と話しました（ともだち、はな）- I talked with a friend
	- 母と映画を見ました（はは、えいが、み）- I watched a movie with my mother

### Register
- と is register-neutral in both uses above — politeness comes from the verb ending, not from と.
- Bare と already means "with" on its own (友達と話しました). いっしょに can be added on top for emphasis (友達といっしょに話しました - I talked together with a friend) — see [[grammar-index/grammar/issho-together-same]] for that combined construction. Plain と without いっしょに, as drafted here, is the simpler, more basic construction and has no dedicated note yet.

### Often confused with
- や (below) — test: is this the complete, exhaustive list of everything involved (と), or just a few representative examples out of a larger set, implying there's more (や)? りんごとみかんを買いました (I bought [only] apples and oranges) vs りんごやみかんを買いました (I bought things like apples and oranges, among other things).

## や - "and" (non-exhaustive listing)

### N1 や N2（や N3...）（＋ など）- "A and B (and so on)"
- Structure
	- N1 + や + N2（+ や + N3...）（+ など）
- Examples
	- このレストランにはうどんやラーメンがあります - this restaurant has udon, ramen, and so on (implying there are other dishes too)
	- 東京や京都や大阪に行きました（とうきょう、きょうと、おおさか）- I went to places like Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, among others
	- 山田さんや小川さんがパーティーに来ました（やまだ、おがわ）- Yamada, Ogawa and others came to the party
	- 動物の中で僕は犬や蛇が好きです（どうぶつ、ぼく、いぬ、へび）- among animals, I like things like dogs and snakes
	- スーパーには飲み物やカップやナプキンがありますか（の、もの）- does the supermarket have things like drinks, cups, napkins?

### Register
- Register-neutral, and slightly more common in written/neutral speech than とか; politeness itself still comes from the sentence's verb ending, not from や.

### Often confused with
- と (above) — test: are you naming the complete, closed set of items (と — this and only this), or picking a few representative examples out of a larger, open set (や — things like this, and probably more)? うどんとラーメンがあります (there is [only] udon and ramen) vs うどんやラーメンがあります (there's udon, ramen, and other things like that).
- とか — [[grammar-index/grammar/tokatoka-enumerate-examples]] — both list a few examples non-exhaustively, but per that file's own register note, とか is casual/spoken (natural in everyday conversation, avoided in formal writing) while や(など) is more neutral and common in written or slightly more formal contexts. てんぷらとかうどんとか (casual, spoken) vs てんぷらやうどんなど (neutral/written).

## どのくらい／ぐらい + かかる - asking approximate duration/cost

### どの＋くらい／ぐらい - "about how much/long" question form, used with かかる
- Structure
	- どの + くらい／ぐらい（＋かかりますか）- asking approximately how long/much something takes; くらい and ぐらい are interchangeable
	- くらい／ぐらい is not exclusive to this idiom — it's a general approximation suffix meaning "about N", and attaches directly to a number/duration on its own (Number + くらい／ぐらい), without どの and without かかる. See [[grammar-index/grammar/vocabulary/Verbs/kakaru]] for かかる itself (N＋が＋かかる or bare Number＋かかる, "to take/cost" time or money) — ぐらい／くらい can attach to those same amounts to make them approximate: 三日間かかる (it takes 3 days) → 三日ぐらいかかる (it takes about 3 days); １０００円かかる (it costs 1000 yen) → １０００円ぐらいかかる (it costs about 1000 yen).
- Examples
	- 駅(えき)から家(うち)までどのくらいかかりますか - about how long does it take from the station to home? → 十分(じゅっぷん)かかります - it takes 10 minutes
	- 東京(とうきょう)から鎌倉(かまくら)までどのくらいかかりますか - about how long does it take from Tokyo to Kamakura? → 一時間(いちじかん)かかります - it takes an hour
	- ここからお寺(てら)までどのくらいかかりますか - about how long from here to the temple?
	- 三日(みっか)ぐらいかかる - it takes about 3 days
	- １０００円(えん)ぐらいかかる - it costs about 1000 yen

### Register
- Register-neutral; くらい and ぐらい are interchangeable in this pattern, with ぐらい slightly more common in casual speech.

### Often confused with
- [[grammar-index/grammar/vocabulary/Verbs/kakaru]] — that file documents the base verb かかる itself ("to cost/take" time or money) as a plain statement (N＋が＋かかる, or a bare number). This entry is specifically about the どのくらい question pattern used to ask about that amount, plus the general くらい／ぐらい approximation suffix — not the verb かかる itself, which is already covered there.

# Vocabulary

## とくい／にがて - good at / poor at (skill sense, register variant of 上手／下手)

#w とくい - good at (something) | more modest/humble way to describe your OWN skill than 上手 — 上手 said about yourself can sound boastful, とくい doesn't
#w にがて - poor at / bad at (something) | skill sense — describes low ability at doing something; marked with が (nouns) or のが (verbs), same as 上手／下手
#w 私(わたし)は日本語(にほんご)がとくいです - I'm good at Japanese
#w 私は歌(うた)うのがとくいです - I'm good at singing
#w 私はドイツ語(どいつご)がにがてです - I'm weak at German
#w 私は漢字(かんじ)を読(よ)むのがにがてです - I'm poor at reading kanji

## にがて - not really into it (preference sense, distinct from the skill sense above)

#w にがて - not really into (something) / not a fan of | preference sense — NOT the same as the skill にがて above; sits between すき and きらい on a liking scale
#w 私(わたし)はビールが{だいすき／すき／にがて／きらい}です - I {love / like / am not really into / dislike} beer

> The two にがて above are the same word, two different functions. Test: is this about ABILITY (can you do it well or not → skill にがて, contrasts with とくい/上手/下手) or about PREFERENCE (do you like it or not → preference にがて, sits on the だいすき/すき/にがて/きらい scale)? 私は漢字を読むのがにがてです (I'm poor AT READING kanji — skill) vs 私はビールがにがてです (I'm not really into beer — preference, close to だんだん嫌い だけど嫌いってほどでもない, "not quite dislike but not keen on it either").
