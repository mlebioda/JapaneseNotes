# 文法
## 。。。ところです - action is about to start / in progress / just finished
→ [[grammar-index/tokoro-desu]]

### 1. V「dict」ところです - about to start
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/tokoro-desu-dict-form]]

**Structure**
- V(dictionary form) + ところです / ところだった

**Comparison**

| Pattern | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ところです | imminent — about to happen any moment now | 食(た)べるところです - I'm about to eat |
| つもりです | intend to — a plan, no implied immediacy | 食(た)べるつもりです - I intend to eat |
| にします | a decision/choice among options, not about timing | これにします - I'll go with this one |

**Examples**
#w これから夕飯(ゆうはん)を食(た)べるところで - Just as I'm about to eat dinner now, ...
#w A: もう荷物(にもつ)は準備(じゅんび)しましたか。 B: 今(いま)から(準備)するところです。 - A: Have you already prepared the luggage? B: I'm just about to (do it) now.
#w 家(いえ)に帰(かえ)ると、妻(つま)が晩(ばん)ご飯(はん)を作(つく)るところだった。 - When I got home, my wife was just about to make dinner.
#w 映画館(えいがかん)に入(はい)ったら、ちょうど映画(えいが)が始(はじ)まるところだった。 - When I entered the movie theater, the movie was just about to start. #teineigo
#w 今(いま)から(準備)するところです。- I'm just about to (prepare it) now. #teineigo
#w 家(いえ)に帰(かえ)ると、妻(つま)が晩(ばん)ご飯(はん)を作(つく)るところだった。 - When I got home, my wife was just about to make dinner.
### 2. V「て形」いるところです - action in progress right now
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/tokoro-desu-teiru-form]]

**Structure**
- V(て form) + いる + ところです

**Comparison**

| Pattern | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ているところです | emphasizes being caught right in the middle of it, live status report | 準備(じゅんび)をしているところです - I'm in the middle of preparing (right now) |
| ている（plain） | general ongoing action or resulting state, no special emphasis on "right now, mid-action" | 準備(じゅんび)をしています - I'm preparing |
| 〜途中(とちゅう) | midway through a process or journey — focuses on incompleteness, not a live report | 準備(じゅんび)の途中(とちゅう)です - It's midway through being prepared |

**Examples**
#w 車(くるま)が急(きゅう)に止(と)まりました。今(いま)原因(げんいん)を調(しら)べているところです。 - The car suddenly stopped. I'm currently in the process of investigating the cause. #teineigo
#w 今(いま)資料(しりょう)の準備(じゅんび)をしているところです。もう少(すこ)しお待(ま)ちください。 - I'm in the middle of preparing the materials right now. Please wait a little longer. #teineigo

## 3. V「た形」ところです - action has just finished
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/tokoro-desu-ta-form]]

**Structure**
- V(た form) + ところです / ところでした

**Comparison**

| Pattern | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| たところです | just finished — this instant, emphasizes the immediacy of completion | 出(で)かけたところです - He just left (this instant) |
| もう + た形 | "already" happened — a plain fact, no emphasis on recency | もう出(で)かけました - He has already left |
| たばかり | "just did" — recent, but looser than ところ; allows a bit more elapsed time | 出(で)かけたばかりです - He just left (recently) |

**Examples**
#w バス乗(の)り場(ば)についたら、バスが出(で)たところでした。 - When I arrived at the bus stop, the bus had just left. #teineigo
#w A: ただいま。 B: お帰(かえ)り。ちょうどご飯(はん)ができたところだよ。 - A: I'm home. B: Welcome back. Dinner's just ready.
#w A: 山田(やまだ)さん、いらっしゃいますか。 B: たった今(いま)出(で)かけたところです。まだ会社(かいしゃ)の近(ちか)くにいると思(おも)います。 - A: Is Yamada-san here? B: He just left. I think he's still near the office. #teineigo
#w たった今(いま)出(で)かけたところです。まだ会社(かいしゃ)の近(ちか)くにいると思(おも)います。B: He/she just left just now. I think he/she is still near the office. #teineigo
### Register
Register: neutral
Politeness comes only from the copula attached after it — だ (casual) vs です/でした (polite). Same shift applies to all three forms: 食べるところだ → 食べるところです, しているところだ → しているところです, 出たところだった → 出たところでした.

## まるで - just like / not at all
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/marude]]

> まるで (from 丸で, "wholly") is an intensifying adverb — it never allows a partial or mild comparison, only total resemblance or total absence.

### Structure

#### Simile use — with よう/みたい (total resemblance)
- まるで + N/clause + ようだ／みたいだ (plain) → まるで夢(ゆめ)のようだ - it's just like a dream
- まるで + N + ような／みたいな (before a noun) → まるで夢(ゆめ)のような話(はなし) - a story just like a dream
- まるで + ように／みたいに (before a verb) → まるで飛(と)ぶように走(はし)った - ran as if flying
- まるで + ようです／みたいです (polite) → まるでアメリカ人(じん)のようです - it's just as if he were American

#### Negation use — with a negative predicate (total absence)
- まるで + [verb/adj]ない → まるでわからない - I don't understand at all
- まるで + 違(ちが)う → まるで違(ちが)う - completely different

#### Examples

#w あの人(ひと)は英語(えいご)がとても上手(じょうず)です。まるでアメリカ人(じん)のようです。 - That person is so good at English — it's just as if he were American

### Register
Register: neutral
まるで carries no politeness of its own — formality comes from whatever follows it (ようだ vs ようです, plain vs polite verb).

## ...ようです
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/you-desu]]
### Structure
1. V「dict」+ ようです
2. いadj + ようです
3. なadj + な + ようです
4. N + の + ようです
### Use cases
#### 1. Similarity - It is like...
> Speaker thinks something or some event looks similar to some other thing

**Examples**
#w あの人(ひと)は英語(えいご)がとても上手(じょうず)です。まるでアメリカ人(じん)のようです。 - That person is so good at English. It's just as if he were American. #teineigo
#w 4月(しがつ)になったのに、まだ寒(さむ)くて、まるで冬(ふゆ)のようです。 - Although it's April, it's still cold — it's just like winter. #teineigo

#w あの難(むずか)しい試験(しけん)に合格(ごうかく)できたのが、本当(ほんとう)に夢(ゆめ)のようです。 - The fact that I passed that difficult exam really feels like a dream. #teineigo
#### 2. Assumption - it seems that
##### ようです vs そうです（様態）
|          | Meaning                                                                                                             | Example                                                                                                                               |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ようです     | An inference from indirect evidence, reasoning, or general knowledge — not necessarily observed directly right now. | 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)るようです - It looks like it's going to rain (e.g. based on a forecast or general reasoning, not necessarily the sky right now) |
| そうです（様態） | A direct, immediate impression from something the speaker is observing right now — not hearsay.                     | 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りそうです - It looks like it's going to rain (the speaker looked at the sky and is speaking from that immediate impression)     |

**Examples**
#w 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)るようです。 - It looks like it's going to rain (indirect inference). #teineigo
#w 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りそうです。 - It looks like it's going to rain (direct visual impression). #teineigo

#w 外(そと)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っているようですね。 - It seems to be raining outside, doesn't it? | use ようだ, not そう(様態): based on indirect signs (sound, wet umbrellas), not watching the sky directly right now #teineigo
#w 山(やま)の上(うえ)は寒(さむ)いようです。 - The top of the mountain seems cold. | use ようだ, not そう(様態): a general/logical inference (altitude, common knowledge), not a direct sensory impression in this moment #teineigo
#w 誰(だれ)でも戦争(せんそう)は嫌(きら)いなようです。 - It seems that everyone dislikes war. | use ようだ, not そう(様態): a generalization about other people's feelings, not a directly observed reaction (そう(様態) would need a visible cue like someone's facial expression) #teineigo

## ようになります / なくなります - become able / stop being able, start to / stop doing
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/you-ni-narimasu-naku-narimasu]]
Expresses a change of state over time — either gaining/losing an ability, or picking up/dropping a habit. Note: よう only appears in the positive form; the negative drops it entirely (なる attaches directly after the ない→く stem).

### Use cases
#### 1. become able / become unable
##### Structure
1. V(potential)「dict」+ ようになる - gained an ability
2. V(potential)「ない形, stem+く」+ なる - lost an ability (no よう)

##### Examples
#w 高校(こうこう)のとき、アメリカに留学(りゅうがく)に行(い)きました。それから英語(えいご)ができるようになりました。 - When I was in high school, I studied abroad in the US. Since then, I became able to speak English. #teineigo
#w 3(さん)ヶ月間(かげつかん)ダンスレッスンに通(かよ)って、上手(じょうず)に踊(おど)れるようになりました。 - I became able to dance well after going to dance lessons for 3 months. #teineigo
#w 年(とし)をとると、小(ちい)さい字(じ)が読(よ)めなくなります。 - As you get older, you become unable to read small letters. #teineigo

#### 2. Start / stop a habit
##### Structure
3. V「dict」+ ようになる - a new habit begins
4. V「ない形, stem+く」+ なる - a habit stops (no よう)

##### Examples
#w 最近(さいきん)自分(じぶん)で料理(りょうり)を作(つく)るようになりました。 - Recently, I've started cooking for myself. (habit) #teineigo
#w 今(いま)までは ローマ字(じ)で日本語(にほんご)を書(か)いていました。でも、最近(さいきん)は漢字(かんじ)で書(か)くようになりました。 - Up until now, I had been writing Japanese in romaji. But recently, I've come to write in kanji. #teineigo
#w このソフトウェアを仕事(しごと)で使(つか)うようになりました。 - I've come to use this software at work. #teineigo
#w 健康(けんこう)のため、最近(さいきん)タバコは吸(す)わなくなりました。 - For the sake of my health, I have recently stopped smoking. #teineigo
#w あの二人(ふたり)はけんかをして、話(はな)さなくなりました。 - Those two had a fight and stopped talking to each other. #teineigo

## ようにします - working hard on / making an effort to
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/you-ni-shimasu]]
The speaker makes an effort (or asks someone else to make an effort) to do — or not do — something.

### Structure
1. V「dict」+ ようにします - try to do something (positive effort)
2. V「ない」+ ようにします - try not to do something (negative effort)
3. V「dict/ない」+ ようにしています - an ongoing, standing commitment to (not) do something
4. V「dict/ない」+ ようにしてください - politer than てください; asks someone to be mindful/careful about an ongoing goal
   5. Not for a direct one-time request or instruction
      1. ケーキを食べるようにしてください - unnatural: eating one specific cake right now is a single concrete act, not an ongoing effort/habit goal — plain ケーキを食べてください fits better here
   6. It's rather for asking someone to keep something in mind going forward

#### Examples
#w 最近(さいきん)運動(うんどう)が足(た)りないので、なるべくたくさん歩(ある)くようにしています。 - Recently, I haven't been getting enough exercise, so I try to walk as much as possible. #teineigo | ongoing habitual effort — not the same as plain 歩いています, which would just state the action without emphasizing deliberate effort

#w 登山(とざん)に行(い)くんだけど、日(ひ)が暮(く)れるまでに、帰(かえ)るようにするよ。心配(しんぱい)しないで。 - I'm going mountain climbing, but I'll make sure to be back before the sun sets. Don't worry. | casual register (よ, plain する) — a personal resolution, not a request directed at someone else

#w 壁(かべ)を塗(ぬ)ったばかりだから、触(さわ)らないようにしてください。 - The wall was just painted, so please be careful not to touch it. #teineigo | softer than 触らないでください — asks for care/mindfulness, not a blunt prohibition

#w 先生(せんせい)は研究中(けんきゅうちゅう)ですので、邪魔(じゃま)をしないようにします。 - Since my teacher is in the middle of research, I'll make sure not to disturb them. #teineigo | first-person resolution/effort, not a command to anyone else

#w 遅刻(ちこく)しないようにしてください。 - Please make an effort not to be late. #teineigo | soft caution, allows for the possibility of slipping — contrast with the next line

#w 遅刻(ちこく)しないでください。 - Please don't be late. #teineigo | direct, blunt prohibition — no effort-framing, expects strict compliance

### Register
ようにします itself carries no fixed politeness — register comes from the copula/verb that follows: する (casual) vs します (polite), してください (polite request) vs plain してくれ/して (casual). All examples above pattern this way.

### Comparison — words/grammar this is easily confused with

| Pattern    | Nuance                                                                                   | Don't confuse with                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 〜ようにする     | speaker's own deliberate, intentional effort/resolution ("I'll make sure to / I try to") | 〜ようになる — a change that happens as a result (gained ability, new habit), not an active decision. ようにする is agentive; ようになる is a resulting state.        |
| 〜ようにしている   | a standing, ongoing practice/habit maintained through effort                             | plain 〜ている — states an ongoing action/state with no implication of deliberate, maintained effort                                                      |
| 〜ようにしてください | polite request to be mindful/careful about a goal, softer, allows some slack             | 〜てください (direct one-time request/instruction) and 〜ないでください (direct blunt prohibition) — both are stricter and don't carry the "make an effort" framing |

## て形 + 欲(ほ)しいです - I want someone (else) to do something
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/te-form-hoshii-desu]]
### Structure
1. V「て形」+ 欲(ほ)しいです - I want someone else to do something

#### Examples
#w みなさん、この漢字(かんじ)はちょっと難(むずか)しいですが、たくさん練習(れんしゅう)して、正(ただ)しく書(か)いてほしいです。 - Everybody, although this kanji is a little difficult, I want you to practice a lot and write it correctly. #teineigo
#w 彼(かれ)はうそをついた。謝(あやま)ってほしい。 - He lied. I want him to apologize.
#w 手伝(てつだ)ってほしいことがあるんだけど…。 - There's something I want you to help me with… (trailing off, inviting a response)

### May be confused with

| Grammar | Meaning | Who performs the action | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| N + が欲(ほ)しいです | I want (a thing) | n/a — desire for an object, not an action | 車(くるま)が欲(ほ)しいです - I want a car |
| V「ます形」+ たいです | I want to do (verb) | the speaker themselves | これを食(た)べたいです - I want to eat this |
| V「て形」+ 欲(ほ)しいです | I want someone else to do (verb) | someone other than the speaker (listener/third party) | 謝(あやま)ってほしい - I want him to apologize |

### Register
欲しい/たい themselves are plain-form adjectives; politeness comes from です (欲しいです, たいです = polite) vs the bare adjective (欲しい, たい = casual), same as any い-adjective. Directing a てほしい want at someone (rather than stating your own たい desire) doesn't change formality — both scale the same way via です.
>Don't use to someone superior to you

###  畳(じょう) - counter for tatami-mat room size
→ [[grammar-index/grammar/jou-tatami-counter]]

> 畳 has two separate readings: たたみ (noun) refers to the physical tatami mat itself, while じょう (counter) measures a room's size by the number of tatami mats it holds. One mat (1畳) is roughly 1.65m², though exact size varies slightly by region (江戸間 vs 京間).

#### Structure

| Number | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1畳 | いちじょう | one-mat (room size) |
| 2畳 | にじょう | two-mat |
| 3畳 | さんじょう | three-mat |
| 4畳半 | よじょうはん | four-and-a-half-mat (classic small room size) |
| 6畳 | ろくじょう | six-mat |
| 8畳 | はちじょう | eight-mat |
| 10畳 | じゅうじょう | ten-mat |
| 何畳 | なんじょう | how many mats? |

Note the irregular reading: 4畳 is よじょう, not よんじょう — the same irregularity seen in 四時(よじ) "4 o'clock."

##### Examples

#w この部屋(へや)は六畳(ろくじょう)です。 - This room is six tatami mats (in size).
#w 六畳一間(ろくじょういちま)のアパートに住(す)んでいます。 - I live in a one-room, six-mat apartment.
#w 四畳半(よじょうはん)の部屋(へや)は狭(せま)いです。 - A four-and-a-half-mat room is small.

##### Register
Register: neutral
畳 as a counter is a plain measurement word with no politeness association — used identically in casual conversation, real-estate listings, and formal writing.
